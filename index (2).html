Frequently Asked Questions

Everything You Might Want to Ask

Answers to the questions prospective clients ask most before hiring an editor or ghostwriter.

What does a developmental editor actually do?
A developmental editor evaluates the big-picture elements of your manuscript — plot structure, pacing, character arcs, point of view, world-building, and thematic consistency. Rather than fixing sentences, a developmental editor helps you fix the story itself, delivering a detailed editorial letter and margin notes before you move to line editing.
What's the difference between developmental editing, line editing, and proofreading?
Developmental editing addresses structure, plot, and character (big-picture). Line editing refines voice, flow, tone, and sentence-level craft. Proofreading is the final pass that catches grammar, spelling, punctuation, and formatting errors before publication. Most manuscripts benefit from all three, in that order.
How much does it cost to hire a book editor?
Editing rates vary by manuscript length, genre complexity, and service type, and are typically quoted per word or as a flat project rate after a sample edit. Message with your word count and genre for an exact, no-obligation quote.
How long does it take to edit a full manuscript?
A full developmental edit typically takes 2-4 weeks depending on manuscript length and complexity, while a line edit or proofread can take 1-2 weeks. Ghostwriting timelines depend on scope and are agreed on before the project starts. Rush turnaround can sometimes be arranged.
Do you sign NDAs for ghostwriting projects?
Yes. Client confidentiality is standard practice for every ghostwriting and editing engagement. An NDA can be signed before any manuscript or story details are shared, and authorship credit terms are agreed upon upfront.
What genres do you edit or ghostwrite?
Fiction across romance, fantasy, thriller, mystery, literary fiction, young adult, sci-fi, and contemporary drama, plus select non-fiction and memoir projects. If your genre isn't listed, ask — most story structures translate across genre lines.
Will I lose creative control over my book?
No. Every edit and every ghostwritten draft is built around your vision, voice, and outline. Feedback rounds and revisions are built into the process so the final manuscript still sounds and feels like you.
Do you offer a sample edit before I commit to a full project?
Yes. A short sample edit (usually 1,000-1,500 words) is available before starting a full manuscript so you can review editing style and compatibility before committing to the complete project.

What file formats and tools do you work with?
Manuscripts are typically edited in Microsoft Word using Track Changes and margin comments, following the Chicago Manual of Style unless a client style guide is specified. Google Docs is also supported for real-time collaboration.
Can you help with a manuscript that isn't finished yet?
Yes. Manuscript coaching and partial-draft consultations are available for authors who are still writing and want structural feedback, an outline review, or help getting unstuck before the draft is complete.
Do you work with first-time authors?
Yes, first-time and debut authors make up a large share of past projects. Feedback is delivered clearly and constructively, with plain-language explanations rather than jargon, so new authors understand exactly what to revise and why.
